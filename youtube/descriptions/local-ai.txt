The confusion starts with a basic misunderstanding: running an app locally isn't the same as running AI locally. Every AI product has two layers — the model (the brain) and the harness (the scaffolding around it, like memory, file access, code execution). If you're running Claude Code or ChatGPT on your desktop but it's calling a cloud API, that's not local AI. For it to count, both the model and the harness need to live on your own machine. I walk through why that distinction matters and why it's the thing most people get wrong.

I also get into why this trend specifically took off in the past few months — it's not one reason, it's two that collided: rising costs and shrinking control over frontier models (including a real incident where access to a major model was suspended overnight for an entire group of users), and open-source models finally closing the performance gap with closed ones. Each played a role, and neither fully explains it alone.

I also cover the real bottleneck most people hit — not setup, but model size and memory — and share a free tool I built to help you figure out what hardware you'd actually need for your use case.

The bigger point of this episode: local AI isn't going to fully replace frontier models, and it's not supposed to. The real shift is toward hybrid systems — frontier models for orchestration and the hard stuff, local models for repetitive, high-volume, or private tasks. Understanding where that line sits is what actually matters, for individuals and companies alike.
