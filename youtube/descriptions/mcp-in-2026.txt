I’ve worked in AI for over a decade, with patents and publications at top AI conferences, and I was a tech lead at AWS building production systems for large enterprises. Now I run my own company, Level Up Labs. I start with what MCP is and why it exists. APIs have been around for years, so why do we need another layer? APIs were built for developers writing deterministic software, and agents need a lot more context before they can use them reliably. MCP standardizes that middle layer.

From there I walk through how it works under the hood (host, client, and server), using one running example: telling an agent to email everyone registered for tomorrow’s event. I also clear up the terms people constantly mix up. MCP, APIs, skills, and plugins aren’t competing ideas. They’re layers of the same system.

Then the part most people are confused about: what actually changed in 2026. There are four big shifts every builder working with MCP should know.

The bigger point: in 2025, MCP was about giving agents a standard way to connect to tools. In 2026, it’s about making those connections robust at scale, with longer-running work, more sensitive data, and companies depending on it to run real operations.
